Dear Editors,

I am submitting "Longitudinal Boundary Sharpness Coefficient Slopes Do Not Improve Prediction
of Alzheimer's Disease Conversion in Mild Cognitive Impairment: A Landmark Survival Analysis in
ADNI with External Validation" for consideration as an Original Article in Brain Communications.

The paper reports a null result and shows where an earlier positive one came from. The Boundary
Sharpness Coefficient is a T1-derived measure of how sharply grey and white matter separate at
the cortical ribbon, and its rate of change has been proposed as an early marker of cortical
degeneration. Analysed the usual way, with follow-up starting at the MCI baseline scan and
features drawn from a subject's whole scan series, BSC slopes predicted conversion at a
cross-validated C-index of 0.72. That design lets features come from scans acquired after the
outcome, so a subject has to survive long enough to supply them. Rebuilt as a landmark analysis
at 24 months, with features restricted to scans acquired at or before the landmark and follow-up
starting there, the same subjects and the same pipeline gave 0.50. Age, sex, education and MMSE
at the landmark reached 0.747, and adding the BSC block moved that by 0.000 (95% CI -0.013 to
+0.013). No individual slope separated converters from non-converters after false discovery
correction. Baseline BSC magnitude also varied with scanner field strength and tracked SNR, which
matters for anyone reading small effects off this measure.

Brain Communications seems right for this on two counts. The journal has published prediction
work on the same question, including Nakagawa and colleagues (2020) on deep survival analysis of
MRI for AD conversion, so the subject is in scope. Beyond that, the contribution here is largely
a correction to a literature that reports strong imaging-based prediction of MCI conversion. A
journal that puts rigour and transparency at the centre of what it publishes is the right place
for a result whose main use is to send other groups back to check their follow-up definitions.
